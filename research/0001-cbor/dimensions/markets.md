# CBOR: markets and demand drivers

CBOR (RFC 8949 / STD 94) has no market of its own — it is a wire format. What
follows sizes the *host* markets/systems that have chosen CBOR (or a CBOR
profile such as COSE/CWT) and explains, market by market, why a binary
encoding was chosen over JSON. Every market-size figure below is a
vendor/analyst estimate unless stated otherwise, and is flagged as such.
Confidence for this whole dimension: **medium** for the regulatory/technical
claims (primary sources checked), **low** for market-size numbers (all are
analyst-firm estimates with undisclosed methodology, and they disagree with
each other by 3-10x in several cases).

## Format competition

Candid, market-by-market assessment of where CBOR wins and loses against protobuf, MessagePack, ASN.1/DER, TLV, Avro, FlatBuffers, and JSON+JWS.

**Where CBOR wins:**
- **IETF/security-protocol ecosystems**: COSE, CWT/EAT, CDDL-specified constrained protocols (CoAP, OSCORE, EDHOC), WebAuthn/CTAP2, RATS attestation. Here CBOR wins largely because the IETF standardized on it as the "binary JSON" companion to JOSE/JWT — it's the path of least resistance for anyone already doing JSON Web Token-style security engineering who needs a binary form, and it's an open IETF standard (RFC 8949 / STD 94) rather than a vendor format, which matters for government/regulatory specs (ISO 18013-5, EUDI Wallet ARF) that need a vendor-neutral, freely implementable encoding.
- **ISO mobile credentials (mdoc/mDL)**: CBOR + COSE is simply *the* specified encoding; there is no competing binary format in that spec.
- **Self-describing, schema-optional binary JSON replacement**: where teams want "JSON semantics, smaller/faster" without committing to a schema-compiler toolchain (unlike protobuf/Avro/FlatBuffers), CBOR and MessagePack compete directly; CBOR's edge is IETF standardization, native tagging (extensibility for dates, bignums, etc.) and its explicit CDDL schema-description language for teams that do want optional schema.

**Where CBOR loses:**
- **High-throughput internal microservices / gRPC**: Protobuf dominates decisively — it *is* the gRPC wire format, has mature multi-language codegen, strong schema evolution guarantees, and a massive existing Google/cloud-native tooling ecosystem. CBOR is essentially absent from this space.
- **Connected-vehicle/cloud telemetry**: search evidence (AWS IoT reference architecture) shows Protobuf, not CBOR, as the default for OEM vehicle telemetry pipelines — strict typing and codegen across huge fleets of numeric signals matter more than CBOR's flexibility.
- **Big-data/analytics pipelines**: Avro (Kafka/Hadoop ecosystems) and Parquet/FlatBuffers-adjacent columnar formats own this space; CBOR is not a contender for large-scale schema-registry-driven data pipelines.
- **Legacy telecom/PKI/X.509**: ASN.1/DER remains locked in for certificates, LDAP, SNMP, and cellular signaling (3GPP) by decades of embedded infrastructure; nobody is migrating existing PKI to CBOR — COSE/CWT is additive (new use cases like CWT-based mdoc), not a DER replacement.
- **Web-facing developer APIs**: JSON (+ JWS/JOSE where signing is needed) remains dominant for anything browser/JS-developer-facing because native `JSON.parse`/`fetch` support and human-debuggability beat CBOR's size/speed advantage when payloads aren't bandwidth- or power-constrained. CBOR only wins once the constraint (radio bandwidth, secure-element RAM, deterministic signing) is real; where it isn't, JSON+JWS keeps the field by default/inertia.
- **Gaming/real-time engines**: FlatBuffers (and Cap'n Proto) win where zero-copy access matters (no decode step at all) — a property CBOR does not have, since it still requires parsing into an in-memory structure.

**Bottom line**: CBOR's genuine, defensible footholds are standards-track IETF/security work (COSE/CWT/EAT) and ISO digital-credential specs (mdoc/mDL, and by extension the EUDI Wallet) — markets chosen *for* CBOR by a standards body, not by competitive benchmarking against protobuf. Outside of that lane, CBOR is one of several viable "binary JSON" options competing with MessagePack on developer mindshare, and it loses to protobuf/Avro/FlatBuffers wherever schema tooling, codegen, or zero-copy performance are the deciding criteria.

## Figures I would not rely on

- **Confidential computing market size for 2026**: analyst estimates span $5.73B to $54B — roughly a 10x spread across Everest Group, Fortune Business Insights, Grand View Research, TBRC, and Mordor Intelligence. Everest Group's implied 90-95% CAGR is not plausible as a sustained compounding rate and looks like a promotional figure. Do not cite any single number from this set as "the" market size.
- **Passwordless authentication market size for 2026**: five analyst firms give five different figures ($16.85B–$27.66B) with no visible shared methodology; treat as "tens of billions, low-to-mid double-digit CAGR claimed" at best.
- **LwM2M market CAGR of 23% by 2027**: sourced to a 2019/2020 PR Newswire press release from Absolute Market Insights; stale, single-source, and typical of syndicated press-release market-sizing that gets re-cited without refresh. Flagged, not relied on.
- **Matter certified-device counts (350+ products, 70+ manufacturers, March 2026)**: these are certification-registry counts, not shipped-unit or installed-base volumes; do not conflate with actual device shipment figures, which were not found in this pass.
- **C2PA "6,000+ members/affiliates"**: a membership/coalition count, not a measure of how many images or videos actually carry C2PA manifests in the wild; treat as an adoption *intent* signal, not a usage figure.
- **Digital identity solutions/verification market figures generally**: every firm defines "digital identity" market scope differently (verification-only vs. wallets vs. broader IAM), so the $14B–$57B range across sources reflects different market definitions as much as different estimates of the same thing.
